Treatment Model for T3C Mental and Behavioral Health Support Services

Evidence-Based, Trauma-Informed Care Framework

📚 Foundational Treatment Model

This Mental and Behavioral Health Support Services treatment model is an enhancement and extension of our foundational TBRI® model used in the T3C Basic Foster Family Home Support Services package.

For comprehensive information about the core TBRI® principles, implementation strategies, education plans, and foundational practices, please refer to:

View Complete TBRI® Resource Guide (Basic Foster Family Home)

All staff and caregivers working with children in the Mental and Behavioral Health package must first complete training in the foundational TBRI® model before receiving specialized mental health training.

1. Treatment Model Framework and Structure

Our Child Placing Agency implements Trust-Based Relational Intervention® (TBRI®) as our primary treatment model, enhanced with clinical interventions for providing the T3C Mental and Behavioral Health Support Services. TBRI provides the evidence-based, trauma-informed foundation while incorporating specialized mental health practices for children with DSM-5 diagnoses requiring intensive therapeutic support.

Core TBRI Principles with Clinical Enhancements

The enhanced TBRI framework maintains three interconnected principles adapted for children with mental health needs. See the foundational TBRI® Resource Guide for detailed information on the core principles.

Connecting Principles - Therapeutic Engagement

  • Therapeutic communication techniques integrated into daily interactions
  • Age-appropriate relationship building strategies
  • Engagement approaches that support trauma recovery
  • Daily check-ins using validated tools
  • Connection maintained during behavioral crises

Empowering Principles - Clinical Support

  • Medication management integrated into routines
  • Sensory accommodations based on individual needs
  • Crisis prevention planning with individualized strategies
  • Relaxation and coping techniques
  • Regular health monitoring

Correcting Principles - Behavioral Interventions

  • Positive Behavioral Interventions and Supports (PBIS) framework
  • Functional assessment of challenging behaviors
  • Individualized behavior support plans
  • Teaching at appropriate developmental level
  • Natural consequences with therapeutic support

Service Integration

Our model integrates required therapeutic services within the foster home environment:

Service Component Description
Therapeutic Services • Weekly individual therapy (minimum)
• Family therapy as clinically indicated
• Group therapy for skill building
• Psychiatric services for medication management
• 24/7 crisis intervention availability
Staffing Structure • Treatment Director meeting T3C qualifications
• Licensed Therapists (1:14 ratio)
• Behavior Support Specialists (1:15 ratio)
• Crisis Management Staff (1:25 ratio)
• Case Managers with mental health training (1:15 ratio)

2. Meeting Custom Physical, Emotional, Social, and Spiritual Well-being Needs

Our enhanced TBRI model addresses all domains of well-being with clinical precision:

Physical Well-being

Children with mental health diagnoses often experience physical symptoms and medication effects requiring careful management:

  • Comprehensive health assessments and monitoring
  • Medication administration and side effect tracking
  • Sleep hygiene programs
  • Physical activity as therapeutic intervention
  • Nutritional planning
  • STAR Health coordination

Emotional Well-being

Intensive emotional support addresses the complex needs of children with mental health diagnoses:

  • Daily mood monitoring
  • Crisis safety planning
  • Emotion regulation skill training
  • Trauma-informed interventions
  • Therapeutic outlets (journaling, arts)
  • CANS assessment every 90 days

Social Well-being

Mental health symptoms significantly impact relationships, requiring structured support:

  • Evidence-based social skills training
  • Supported peer interactions
  • Family therapy and education
  • School collaboration
  • Community integration with support

Spiritual Well-being

  • Values development at appropriate level
  • Cultural identity support
  • Inclusion in faith communities when desired
  • Meaning-making opportunities

3. Evidence and Data Informing Treatment Model Selection

🏆 Evidence-Based Recognition

California Evidence-Based Clearinghouse for Child Welfare (CEBC) Rating:

TBRI® Caregiver Training is rated as a "Promising Practice" based on scientific research showing improved outcomes for children who experienced trauma, abuse, and neglect.

Title IV-E Prevention Services Clearinghouse:

TBRI® is recognized for mental health prevention and treatment, making it eligible for use in programs requiring evidence-based, trauma-informed interventions.

Implementation Support:

The Karyn Purvis Institute of Child Development (KPICD) provides ongoing support including coaching, site visits, teleconferencing, and email support to ensure fidelity to the model.

Fidelity Measures:

TBRI® includes "Rate Your Understanding" assessments to ensure consistent delivery across all caregivers and settings.

Research Foundation

The integration of TBRI with clinical interventions is supported by substantial evidence. Studies demonstrate that combining attachment-based approaches with evidence-based mental health treatment produces superior outcomes compared to either approach alone. Texas-specific data shows children in programs using integrated models experience:

  • Reduced psychotropic medication use
  • Improved school functioning
  • Fewer behavioral health crises
  • Higher rates of successful transition to less intensive services

Quality Monitoring

Our Continuous Quality Improvement process ensures effective service delivery through:

  • CANS assessments every 90 days
  • Regular review of behavioral incidents
  • Service utilization tracking
  • Foster parent feedback
  • Educational progress monitoring

4. Trauma-Informed Approach for Children Who Have Experienced Abuse and Neglect

Children with mental health diagnoses and trauma histories require specialized approaches that address both experiences. Our model recognizes that trauma often underlies or exacerbates mental health symptoms.

Note: All children and caregivers in the Mental and Behavioral Health package receive the foundational TBRI® education outlined in the Basic Foster Family Home TBRI® Resource Guide, plus additional specialized training in mental health symptom management and crisis intervention.

Assessment and Treatment Integration

  • CANS 3.0 serves as primary assessment tool
  • Treatment planning addresses both trauma and mental health symptoms
  • Evidence-based approaches adapted for individual needs
  • Regular reassessment guides treatment adjustments

Caregiver Support

Foster parents receive comprehensive preparation for this complex population:

  • Training in trauma responses and mental health symptoms
  • Strategies for therapeutic communication
  • Crisis intervention techniques
  • 24/7 consultation availability

5. Appropriateness of the Treatment Model for T3C Mental and Behavioral Health Support Services

Service Package Alignment

Our enhanced TBRI model directly addresses T3C requirements:

T3C Requirement Our Approach
Regular individual/family therapy Weekly individual therapy minimum; family as indicated
Service planning every 90 days CANS-informed reviews with clinical team
24/7 crisis response On-call clinical staff with TBRI training
Evidence-informed treatment TBRI enhanced with clinical interventions
STAR Health coordination Dedicated staff for service navigation

Quality Assurance Process

90-Day Review Components:

  1. CANS 3.0 administration
  2. Progress toward Service Plan goals
  3. Medication effectiveness
  4. Educational functioning
  5. Behavioral data analysis
  6. Continued stay determination

Step-Down Criteria:

  • Improved CANS scores
  • Behavioral stability
  • Medication stabilization
  • Coping skill demonstration
  • Foster parent confidence

Expected Outcomes

Based on evidence-based practices, we anticipate:

Short-Term (3-6 months)

  • Reduced crisis incidents
  • Improved therapy engagement
  • Medication compliance
  • CANS score improvement
  • School stabilization

Long-Term (12-18 months)

  • Placement stability
  • Reduced hospitalizations
  • Decreased medication needs
  • Successful transition to Basic Services when appropriate

Through this enhanced treatment model, our T3C Mental and Behavioral Health Support Services program provides intensive, evidence-based care within TBRI's trauma-informed framework. The integration ensures children receive comprehensive mental health support while maintaining the relational foundation essential for healing.
